Honest counter-point (from verification): AMD’s open-software advantage is contested in practice, not settled. The claim that AMD’s MI325X/MI300X beat all NVIDIA GPUs on perf/$ for low-latency inference was refuted 0-3; a “ROCm ~25% model-accuracy-fail” claim was a contested 1-2 non-survivor. Net: ROCm-vs-CUDA real-world maturity is open in both directions — assert neither parity nor breakage.

Future scope & why Supermicro benefits /deep-research

Status and forward signals (cited)

  • MI400 (2026) 已發表 · 部署待驗證 — 432 GB HBM4, 19.6 TB/s, 40 PF FP4, 10× MoE inference.
  • rack (2026) 已發表 · 部署待驗證 — EPYC “Venice” (Zen 6) + MI400/MI450 + Pensando “Vulcano” NICs + ROCm; 72 GPUs, 260 TB/s, ; on track for 2H 2026.
  • MI500 (2027) and an annual cadence signalled.

The prediction (analytical)

The chip is now the rack: unified CPU+GPU+DPU+ROCm (). Agentic / MoE inference — many-model, memory-hungry — rewards AMD’s memory-capacity + scale-up bet. + + push value away from the proprietary rack (NVL72) toward open, multi-vendor rack integration — exactly where system builders compete. Edge/IoT was not central to this data-center event; treat edge claims as thin here.

Why Supermicro benefits — now sourced (post-event red-team). The event set didn’t name Supermicro, but post-event primary sources close the gap.
