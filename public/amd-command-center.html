執行判決 · Macro Verdict

結論：2026 並非自然勝局；突破口在液冷機櫃交付

Why this verdict?

  1. AMD's memory-capacity lead is clear, but it is only one advantage

    Published roadmap specifications

    EvidenceMI400 lists 432 GB of HBM4; Vera Rubin lists 288 GB, giving AMD 50% more capacity.

    What it meansThis proves a larger per-GPU memory specification, not an across-the-board lead in performance, latency, or TCO.

  2. NVIDIA lists higher per-GPU bandwidth and FP4 figures

    Vendor specifications

    EvidenceMI400 lists 19.6 TB/s and 40 PF FP4; Rubin lists 22 TB/s and 50 PF NVFP4 inference.

    What it meansNVIDIA is higher on the published figures, but precision definitions differ. Without a matched benchmark, this is not a real-workload verdict.

  3. Specifications have not yet become a delivered, validated win

    Timeline and evidence open

    EvidenceNVIDIA reports Vera Rubin in full production; AMD/HPE target Helios customer deployment in 2H/late 2026. Broad GA, ramp speed, supply scope, and ROCm-versus-CUDA maturity remain unresolved.

    What it meansUntil supply, delivery dates, and like-for-like tests are confirmed, an AMD announcement is not a free win and does not justify an irreversible capacity bet.

  4. Supermicro controls liquid-cooled rack delivery

    Commercial inference

    EvidenceSupermicro is an announced Helios collaborator and reports shipping complete liquid-cooling solutions for more than 100,000 GPUs per quarter; neither fact proves a Helios design win or shipment.

    What it meansChip vendors set the GPU specifications. Supermicro can differentiate on DLC, rack integration, validation, supply, delivery, and service SLAs. Open standards also ease ODM entry, so the proof must be in delivery.

    View the Helios rack architecture and evidence boundary

Evidence boundary The specifications, full-production statement, and liquid-cooling volume above are vendor-sourced. No matched independent benchmark exists for the two next-generation racks; Helios collaboration is not a named design win or shipment.

Decision implication

Win the design and validation work before committing capacity

First confirm named OEMs, lock the reference design, and complete same-workload benchmarks plus ROCm validation. Make an irreversible capacity commitment only after broad GA, supply, and ramp are clear.
